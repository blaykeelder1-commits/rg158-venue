Executive Summary

The play. Bootstrap a curated comedy series from $10,000 starting capital. Each weekend's net funds the next weekend's talent and operations, snowballing working capital from $10K (July) to $400K+ (February) — funding the deposits, production, and marketing for the April 2027 country music festival without external financing.

Why comedy first. Comedy is the lowest-friction, highest-margin live entertainment category for a 200-cap room. One mic. One stool. No band rigs. Comics carry their own audiences via TikTok, Instagram, and podcasts. Two-show nights double the room without doubling overhead. Comedy ticket sales are recession-resilient.

Strategy: snowball, not splurge. With $10K starting capital, we cannot afford a Tier A anchor in months 1–4. Instead we open with low-fee, high-ROI bookings: Heath Hyche (Birmingham AL native, Blue Collar TV alum), local Mobile/Pensacola circuit, Adam Minnick (Dry Bar/Nateland). We compound revenue into August/September with mid-tier names (Aaron Weber, Phil Hanley, Lace Larrabee). By November our working capital supports our first Tier A anchor (Killer Beaz). January and February deliver back-to-back anchors, with Trae Crowder's February show doubling as the country festival announcement event.

The math works because of ROI ranking. Every comedian on this lineup is selected by an explicit "gate ÷ fee" ratio. Heath Hyche projects a 3.6 ratio. Aaron Weber 2.94. Mark Chalifoux 3.15. Phil Hanley 1.77 (he commands premium ticket prices, not just volume). Tony Baker 1.85. The bang-for-buck candidates carry the volume; anchors deliver the brand and the email-list compounding.

The capital sequencing rule

If working capital is...Maximum talent fee per bookingTier accessible

Why this rule: a Tier A anchor that fails at 60% sellthrough loses ~$8K. If we book a Tier A while working capital is below $80K, one bad weekend kills the series. With $400K+ working capital, the same loss is ~2% of bank — recoverable inside one weekend.

Appendix A · Veteran Promoter Review — Critical Risks

Read by an experienced promoter/event-specialist lens before going live. The strategy is structurally sound and the goal is achievable, but five mathematical risks can kill it and thirteen operational gaps will quietly bleed money if not fixed before show one.

A.1 · The 5 risks that can kill this — fix before booking anything

Risk 1 · Two-show nights at announce is the single biggest assumption risk.
A 200-cap room × 2 shows = 400 paid seats per Friday. For a market with zero comedy track record, that's a 12-month-out goal, not a launch-night goal. Helium, Zanies, and the Improv chains all announce one show first; second show is added "due to demand" only after sellouts.

Fix: First 90 days = 1-show nights only. Add the second show only on shows where the first is 80%+ sold 14 days out. Conservative net forecast drops to $220K–$280K on a 1-show ramp, not the v2 $384K.

Risk 2 · 60% sellthrough is the industry sellout rate, not the average.
Industry baseline for a brand-new venue is 35–50% in months 1–3, climbing to 55–65% only by month 4–6. If July does 40% on a $5K talent fee, you net $1K–$2K — not $7K–$13K. Snowball stalls before show 3.

Fix: Stress-test the snowball at 40% sellthrough. If it breaks, the lineup needs more $1.5K–$2.5K openers stacked before the first $5K booking.

Risk 3 · $10K cannot support a $5K talent fee — the working-capital math is brutal.
$5K talent + $1.5K production + $400 insurance + $1K staff + $400 marketing + $300 hospitality + $300 contingency = $8,900 in costs before a single ticket sells. That leaves $1,100 of cushion for a delayed Square payout, refund batch, or Saturday no-show.

Fix: Show 1 talent ceiling = $1,500–$2,000 max. v2 already places Heath Hyche there — keep it. Don't book a $5K headliner until working capital ≥ $20K liquid in the festival sub-account.

Risk 4 · Festival venue gap is the critical path, not Phase 2.
No 10K-attendee outdoor venue is locked. Permitting an outdoor 10K event in a small Alabama city takes 6–9 months: city council, county zoning, ABC liquor variance, fire marshal capacity, EMS staging, traffic plan, neighbor notification. Without venue secured by September 2026, you cannot sign a Parmalee deposit, bind insurance, lock production, or pitch sponsors.

Fix: Saraland city manager meeting this week. Plan A (Saraland Sports Complex / Greater Gulf State Fairgrounds) and Plan B (private 60–80 acre pasture with road access) both scoped by end of June 2026.

Risk 5 · Talent quote inflation 2026 is real — your locks aren't locks.
Pollstar / Ticket Fairy 2026 data shows 30–50% YoY fee climbs on Tier B/C touring comics. A May 2026 verbal quote for an October 2026 show typically does not hold — agents re-quote on Netflix specials, viral clips, or tour announcements.

Fix: Every quote must be (a) written, (b) explicitly date-bracketed ("valid for shows performed by [date]"), (c) deposit-locked as fast as cash flow allows. Wyatt Flores especially: lock his quote this week, not May/June.

C.5 · Hard lines — never negotiate these away

  • Bar / F&B revenue — 100% stays with venue. Always.
